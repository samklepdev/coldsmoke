# Coldsmoke — Brand, Product & Business Plan

*A masculine/unisex cologne built on a cool-spice-and-smoke scent profile.*

> **Read me first.** This is a working plan, not legal or financial advice. Two gates control real spending: (1) a **USPTO trademark clearance** in Class 3 (a search-firm or attorney search — not the informal marketplace check that produced this doc), and (2) **written quotes from a fragrance house and a co-packer**, which replace every cost estimate below with real numbers. Treat all figures here as illustrative placeholders to pressure-test, not budgets.

## Part 1 — Brand Identity

### Concept
Coldsmoke is a cool-on-the-surface, dark-underneath scent: a crisp icy opening that burns down into spice, musk, and a wisp of smoke. The name reads two ways on purpose — "cold smoke" is the skier's term for the lightest, driest powder snow, and it literally describes the scent arc (cold up top, smoke in the base). The brand leans quiet-confident and unisex-masculine, not loud "bro" fragrance.

### Positioning statement
> For people who want a distinctive daily scent without a designer price tag, Coldsmoke is an inspired-by-the-classics cologne that opens cold and dries down dark — familiar enough to love on first spray, original enough to be its own thing.

### Tagline options
- *Cold air. Dark spice.*
- *Cool on the surface. Smoke underneath.*
- *Sharp, dark, unforgettable.*
- *Wear the chill.*
- *Frost with an edge.*

### Scent profile (note pyramid)
Interpreting the "cool spice / dark musk / fresh top" family into an ownable Coldsmoke composition:

| Layer | Notes | Role |
|-------|-------|------|
| **Top** | Bergamot, iced spearmint, black & pink pepper | The cold, bright first impression |
| **Heart** | Cardamom, clary sage, a whisper of cinnamon, lavender | The spice that warms as it opens |
| **Base** | Dark musk, amber, cedarwood, patchouli, smoky vetiver | The lingering "smoke" and skin-scent dry-down |

> Sourced as a **"Black Ice type" fragrance oil** to anchor the familiar profile, ideally with a **custom modification** from the fragrance house so the final scent isn't identical to every other brand buying the same stock oil. The public scent story references *only* the notes above — never the trademarked air-freshener name.

### Visual & packaging direction
- **Bottle:** heavy rectangular glass, matte-black or frosted finish, gunmetal/brushed-silver collar and cap.
- **Wordmark:** minimal wide-set sans serif; optional single icon (a rising wisp / ember).
- **Outer box:** black or kraft rigid box, silver foil or spot-UV wordmark.
- **Feel:** cold, mineral, premium-minimal — closer to a niche house than a drugstore shelf.

### Brand voice
Spare and dry. Short sentences. No exclamation-point energy. Confidence through restraint — the packaging and the name do the talking.

## Part 2 — How to Make It (Production Guide)

### A note on strength
The formula below is an **eau de toilette / light-cologne** concentration (~10% fragrance). Drop the oil to ~4–6% for a lighter "splash cologne," or raise toward the IFRA leave-on cap for a stronger EDT. **Never exceed the IFRA maximum on your fragrance oil's certificate** — that cap is the safety ceiling for a leave-on skin product.

### Ingredients & materials
- **Perfumer's alcohol** (denatured, cosmetic-grade) or 190-proof (95%) ethanol
- **"Black Ice type" fragrance oil** — cosmetic/skin-safe grade, **with its IFRA certificate**
- **Distilled water**
- **Vegetable glycerin** (mild fixative/emollient)
- Glass beakers, graduated cylinders, glass stirring rod, digital scale (0.01 g)
- Coffee filters or lab filter paper + funnel
- Amber glass bottles for maceration; final atomizer bottles
- Nitrile gloves, labels, batch-record sheet

### Master formula
**Per 100 ml (EDT strength):**

| Ingredient | Volume | % |
|---|---|---|
| Perfumer's alcohol / 95% ethanol | 70 ml | 70% |
| Distilled water | 18 ml | 18% |
| "Black Ice type" fragrance oil | 10 ml | 10% |
| Vegetable glycerin | 2 ml | 2% |

**Scaled to a pilot batch of 50 × 50 ml bottles (2.5 L nominal):**

| Ingredient | Volume |
|---|---|
| Perfumer's alcohol / 95% ethanol | 1,750 ml |
| Distilled water | 450 ml |
| "Black Ice type" fragrance oil | 250 ml |
| Vegetable glycerin | 50 ml |

> Add ~5–10% overage to every quantity to cover filtration and filling losses.

### Step-by-step process
1. **Sanitize** all glassware and workspace; wear gloves. Work in a cool, well-ventilated room away from flame (ethanol is flammable).
2. **Dissolve the fragrance oil into the alcohol first.** Pour the alcohol, add the fragrance oil, and stir until fully clear and combined. (Oil must go into alcohol, not water.)
3. **Add distilled water slowly**, stirring. A slight cloudiness (louche) can appear and usually clears during maceration; if it stays very cloudy, nudge the water down slightly next batch.
4. **Add glycerin** and stir to combine.
5. **Macerate:** seal in amber glass, label with batch # and date, and rest **2–4 weeks** in a cool, dark place. This lets the blend "marry" and mellow — skipping it leaves the scent sharp and alcoholic.
6. **Cold-filter:** run the macerated liquid through coffee/lab filter paper to remove any haze or particulate.
7. **Fill & cap** into atomizer bottles. Crimp/seal, label, and box.
8. **Rest again** a few days before shipping if possible.

### Quality, safety & records (non-negotiable)
- **Patch test every new formula:** dab on inner forearm, wait 24–48 h, check for redness/itching before wider use.
- **Batch records:** log batch #, date, exact quantities, fragrance-oil lot #, and yield for every run. This is both good practice and your defense if anyone ever reports a reaction.
- **Keep the IFRA cert and a cosmetic safety assessment on file** for each product (see regulatory section).
- **Batch-code the bottles** so any unit traces back to its record.

### Scaling path
Hand-blending is fine to validate demand and dial in the scent. Once orders are steady, move to a **contract manufacturer / co-packer** — they give you batch consistency, accurate fills, and (importantly) they become the registered manufacturing facility instead of you. See regulatory section.

## Part 3 — Business Plan

### Executive summary
Coldsmoke is a direct-to-consumer cologne brand selling an inspired-by, cool-spice-and-smoke fragrance at an accessible price. It launches lean — small pilot batches, DTC via Shopify, and local/market testing — then moves to a co-packer as volume grows. The edge is **brand and design**, not a secret scent: the profile is deliberately familiar, so differentiation comes from name, packaging, story, and positioning.

### Product line (phased)
- **Launch:** one hero scent — Coldsmoke EDT, 50 ml.
- **Phase 2:** travel size (10–15 ml), and a 100 ml.
- **Phase 3:** a second scent to become a "house," plus optional adjacent SKUs (beard oil, solid cologne) that reuse the same fragrance oil.

### Market & customer
- **Who:** 18–40, value-conscious fragrance buyers who like "smells like [designer]" scents but want something with its own identity; gift buyers.
- **Where they are:** TikTok/Instagram fragrance community ("FragTok"), Reddit fragrance communities, local markets and pop-ups.
- **Why they buy:** accessible price, distinctive look, a scent that reads premium.

### Competitive landscape
The "inspired-by / dupe" fragrance space is large and growing (Dossier, Alt. Fragrances, ALT, plus countless indie makers and Middle-Eastern houses like Armaf, Lattafa, Maison Alhambra). Implications:
- **Table stakes:** a familiar, high-performing scent at a fair price.
- **Where you win or lose:** brand, packaging, story, and community — because the scent alone is not ownable.
- **Avoid:** competing on "cheapest dupe." Compete on identity.

### Business model & pricing (illustrative)
- **Channel:** DTC (Shopify) first; wholesale/consignment to local shops second.
- **Target retail:** ~$39–$49 for 50 ml (inspired-by/artisan tier).
- **Wholesale:** ~50% of retail.

> Margins look healthy on paper; the real costs are **customer acquisition (ads), shipping, returns, and compliance**. Model those before celebrating the gross margin.

### Regulatory & legal checklist (US)
> Verify all of this against current FDA guidance and, ideally, a professional — cosmetics law changed materially under **MoCRA (Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act, 2022)** and rules are still phasing in.

- [ ] **Trademark:** clear "Coldsmoke" in Class 3 via USPTO TESS + attorney search; confirm the apparel/agency/dispensary uses in other classes don't block you. Do **not** use "Black Ice" or any leetspeak variant as a name.
- [ ] **Business formation:** LLC (liability protection matters for skin products), EIN, business bank account.
- [ ] **Product liability insurance** before selling a single bottle.
- [ ] **Safety substantiation:** maintain a cosmetic safety assessment (CPSR/tox review) + IFRA compliance docs on file per product. *Required even for small/exempt businesses — there is no exemption from safety substantiation or adverse-event reporting.*
- [ ] **Responsible Person + labeling:** the entity named on the label is the legal "Responsible Person"; labels need an ingredient list (descending order of predominance), net quantity, business name/address, warnings, and a **US adverse-event contact**.
- [ ] **Facility registration / product listing:** businesses under **$1M average annual gross sales** (3-yr avg) making non-high-risk products are generally **exempt** from facility registration and product listing. Cologne is not high-risk, so a small launch likely qualifies. **Using a co-packer** shifts facility registration to them regardless.
- [ ] **Watch the pending rules:** GMP and **fragrance-allergen labeling** are not final as of early 2026 — build your formula docs assuming allergen disclosure is coming.

### Operations & supply chain
- **Formulation:** fragrance house for the "Black Ice type" oil + custom mod (get the IFRA cert).
- **Production:** DIY pilot → co-packer at volume.
- **Packaging:** bottles/atomizers/boxes from packaging suppliers (Berlin Packaging, SKS Bottle, or overseas at volume — mind MOQs and lead times).
- **Fulfillment:** self-ship at first; 3PL once volume justifies it.

### Marketing & sales
- **Content-first:** short-form video (the scent journey, the making-of, the cold-smoke aesthetic). The category lives on TikTok/IG.
- **Sampling:** cheap 2 ml samples / discovery sets lower the risk of buying a scent unsniffed — critical for an unknown brand.
- **Community:** seed to fragrance micro-influencers; engage Reddit fragrance communities honestly (no astroturfing).
- **Local proof:** markets and pop-ups to watch real reactions before big production runs.
- **Retention:** email/SMS, restock flows, and a second scent to drive repeat purchase.

### Financial outlook (illustrative)
- **Pilot:** ~50–100 units to validate scent, packaging, and demand. Low four figures all-in.
- **Break-even:** driven mostly by ad efficiency (CAC) and AOV, not COGS. Model CAC:LTV before scaling spend.
- **Reinvest** early profit into a second scent and better packaging rather than pulling cash out.

### Milestones / roadmap
1. **Clear the name** (USPTO + domain/handles) — *gate before spending on packaging.*
2. **Lock the scent** — order the Black Ice type oil, dial in the formula, patch test.
3. **Safety + entity** — LLC, insurance, safety assessment, compliant label.
4. **Pilot batch + brand assets** — 50–100 units, Shopify store, content.
5. **Soft launch** — DTC + local markets; gather reviews and reactions.
6. **Iterate & scale** — move to co-packer, add SKUs, expand channels.

### Risks & mitigations
| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Trademark conflict on "Coldsmoke" | Formal Class 3 clearance before spend; have a backup name in the same lane |
| Scent doesn't stand out / weak performance | Custom mod from the fragrance house; test longevity/projection before launch |
| High ad costs eat margin | Lean on organic content + sampling; measure CAC:LTV early |
| Compliance gap (MoCRA/labeling) | Safety docs + compliant label from day one; co-packer offloads facility registration |
| Skin reaction / liability | Patch testing, IFRA compliance, batch records, product liability insurance |

## Part 4 — Product Label Specification

> A compliant US cosmetic label for the 50 mL EDT. Brackets are placeholders to fill once the LLC and address exist. Two pieces indie labels most often get wrong — the **adverse-event contact** and **Responsible Person** lines — are worth a compliance-service or attorney once-over. Don't finalize the ingredient line until you have the fragrance oil's **allergen certificate** in hand; the exact allergen tail is supplier-specific.

**On-label URL decision:** print **`wearcoldsmoke.com`** on the bottle (reads as the brand). Use `getcoldsmoke.com` for ad campaigns and QR codes, redirecting to the store.

### Front — Principal Display Panel (PDP)
Two legally required elements: the **statement of identity** ("Eau de Toilette Spray") and the **net quantity** in metric + US customary. Everything else is brand.

```
            COLDSMOKE
        Eau de Toilette Spray

         Cold air. Dark spice.

         50 mL  /  1.7 FL OZ
```

### Back / Side — Information Panel
Four required blocks:

**1. Ingredients** — INCI names, descending order of predominance:
```
INGREDIENTS: Alcohol Denat., Aqua (Water), Parfum (Fragrance),
Glycerin[, + fragrance allergen components per supplier
certificate — e.g., Limonene, Linalool, Coumarin, Citral].
```

**2. Responsible Person** — the entity named here is legally accountable:
```
Distributed by Coldsmoke LLC, [Street], [City, ST ZIP], USA
```
or, once on a co-packer:
```
Manufactured for Coldsmoke LLC, [City, ST ZIP], USA
```

**3. Adverse-event contact** — required on-label since Dec 29, 2024:
```
Questions or to report an adverse event:
care@wearcoldsmoke.com  ·  [US phone]  ·  [US mailing address]
```

**4. Warnings** — standard for an alcohol-based leave-on fragrance:
```
FLAMMABLE. Keep away from heat, flame, and direct sunlight.
For external use only. Avoid contact with eyes; if contact
occurs, rinse thoroughly. Discontinue use if irritation occurs.
Keep out of reach of children.
```

**Brand line:** `wearcoldsmoke.com`

### Also print (best practice; some required for retail)
- **Batch/lot code** — ties each bottle to its batch record.
- **PAO symbol** (open-jar icon, e.g. "12M") — expected on fragrance; required for EU/UK.
- **UPC/barcode** — needed for any retail/wholesale.
- **Recyclability mark** for glass/box.

### Print-ready notes for the designer
- Add **3 mm bleed** on all sides; keep text **≥3 mm** from trim (safe area).
- Convert type to **outlines**; supply as vector (PDF/SVG/AI).
- Build color in **CMYK or spot** (a silver/metallic spot ink suits the gunmetal direction) — not RGB.
- Swap the placeholder sans serif for the final brand typeface.
